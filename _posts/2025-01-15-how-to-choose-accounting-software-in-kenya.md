Most Kenyan SMEs start on spreadsheets. Some stay there for years. The switch to proper software usually comes when one of three things happens: a KRA audit, an investor conversation, or a VAT return that took three weeks to assemble.

This guide is what to look for when that moment arrives.

## Start with KRA compliance

Any accounting software for a Kenyan business must handle the local tax system out of the box. If it does not, you are buying a system that will require constant manual adjustments.

Check for:

- **Pre-loaded Kenyan chart of accounts.** If you have to build one from scratch, you have the wrong tool.
- **Automatic 16% VAT** on invoices and purchases, with input tax offset against output tax.
- **KRA PIN storage** against your business profile, so invoices carry the right details.
- **iTax-ready exports** for VAT3 filing.
- **PAYE, SHIF, NSSF, and Housing Levy** if you have employees.

If any of those are missing, walk away.

## Confirm M-Pesa handling

Almost every Kenyan business takes M-Pesa. The software needs to handle it properly, which means:

- Recording the M-Pesa transaction code against each payment, not just "M-Pesa" as a method
- Reconciling against your Safaricom statement at month end
- Separating business M-Pesa from personal M-Pesa in the books

Some tools try to integrate directly with M-Pesa APIs. That is not necessary for most businesses. Manual recording with proper reconciliation works fine and avoids the connection headaches.

## Look at the payroll module

If you employ anyone, payroll is where the biggest compliance headaches live. The monthly statutory filings are easy to miss and expensive to fix.

Good payroll software calculates PAYE, SHIF, NSSF, and Housing Levy automatically, generates payslips, and produces the P10 return and statutory schedules.

If you are doing payroll in Excel, you are one mistake away from a KRA penalty.

## Check the reporting

The software must generate, without manual intervention:

- Profit and loss
- Balance sheet
- Cash flow
- VAT summary
- Aged receivables and payables
- Trial balance

If any of those require you to export to Excel and build the report yourself, it is not doing its job.

## Factor in the price

Kenyan accounting software runs between KES 1,500 and KES 15,000 per month depending on features and user count. Watch for:

- **Setup fees** that are not disclosed upfront
- **Per-user charges** that add up as you grow
- **Payroll as an extra module** when it should be included
- **Annual price increases** with no notice

Ask for the full price list before you commit.

## Test the interface

Have your accountant and your front desk person use it for a week before you buy. If they find it difficult, no feature list will save you. Adoption is the single biggest reason software fails in small businesses.

## The checklist

When evaluating any tool, ask:

1. Does it handle Kenyan VAT, PAYE, SHIF, NSSF, and Housing Levy?
2. Does it handle M-Pesa payments properly?
3. Can I run payroll from it, or is payroll separate?
4. Does it produce all the reports KRA and my bank will ask for?
5. What is the full annual cost, including all users and modules?
6. Can my team actually use it, or will they resist?

If you can answer yes to all six, you have found your tool.
