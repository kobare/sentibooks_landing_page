If your business has crossed KES 5 million in annual turnover, VAT registration with KRA is mandatory. And for many business owners, the process feels like a maze of acronyms: VAT3, iTax, input tax, output tax, PIN certificates.

This guide cuts through it.

## When you must register

KRA requires VAT registration when your taxable turnover hits **KES 5,000,000 in any 12-month period**. Once registered, you must:

- Charge 16% VAT on all taxable supplies
- File VAT returns (Form VAT3) by the 20th of the following month
- Remit the net VAT due to KRA by the same deadline

If you are below the threshold, you can still register voluntarily. This is often useful if your main customers are VAT-registered businesses who want to claim input tax on your invoices.

## What counts as taxable

For most Kenyan businesses, this covers nearly everything you sell:

- Goods sold, standard-rated at 16%
- Services provided, standard-rated at 16%
- Digital products and subscriptions, standard-rated at 16%

Some items are zero-rated (exports, for example) and some are exempt (unprocessed agricultural products). Check the KRA VAT Act if you deal in anything unusual.

## Input VAT: what you can claim

Every purchase you make from a VAT-registered supplier comes with input VAT you can offset against your output VAT. To claim it, you need:

- A valid ETR receipt or tax invoice showing the supplier's PIN
- Proof the purchase was for the business

**Disallowed input VAT** includes fuel, entertainment, and personal expenses. The fuel rule catches a lot of businesses, especially those with vehicles that serve both business and personal use.

## Filing calendar

Set recurring reminders for these dates every month:

| Day | Task |
|-----|------|
| 1 to 5 | Reconcile the previous month's sales and purchases |
| 6 to 10 | Match supplier invoices to ETR receipts |
| 11 to 15 | Prepare the VAT3 return |
| 16 to 19 | Review and file on iTax |
| 20 | Payment deadline |

Missing the 20th triggers automatic penalties of **KES 10,000 or 5% of the tax due, whichever is higher**.

## Common mistakes

Three patterns trigger KRA attention faster than anything else:

1. **Claiming input VAT with no ETR receipt.** Common with cash purchases. If there is no receipt, you cannot claim it.
2. **Filing nil returns while making visible sales.** KRA cross-checks against M-Pesa and bank data. Nil returns with visible income is a red flag.
3. **Mismatching VAT3 sales against income tax turnover.** If the numbers do not reconcile, expect questions.

Any one alone can be explained. Two or more together is an audit invitation.

## Recording VAT in your books

Every invoice you issue and every purchase you make needs to record:

- The VAT-exclusive amount
- The VAT amount
- The VAT-inclusive total
- The supplier's or customer's PIN

If your software does this automatically, you can export a VAT summary at month end that maps directly to Form VAT3. If you are doing this in Excel, it will eventually go wrong.
