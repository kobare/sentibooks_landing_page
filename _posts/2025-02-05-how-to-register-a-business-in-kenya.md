Registering a business in Kenya has become dramatically easier since the process moved to eCitizen. A sole proprietorship takes a few hours. A limited company takes a few days. Here is how each works.

## Sole proprietorship vs limited company

**Sole proprietorship** is simpler and cheaper. You register a business name, get a KRA PIN, and start trading. All profits are taxed as personal income, and you are personally liable for business debts.

**Limited company** costs more and requires more paperwork. In exchange, the company is a separate legal entity. Your personal assets are protected from business liabilities, and the tax rate is fixed at 30 percent for resident companies.

Most Kenyan small businesses start as sole proprietorships and convert to limited companies when they need liability protection, corporate clients insist on it, or turnover crosses a threshold where corporate tax becomes cheaper than personal rates.

## Registering a business name on eCitizen

1. Log in to eCitizen with your ID and phone number.
2. Go to the Business Registration Service portal.
3. Search the name you want to use. This checks if it is already taken.
4. Reserve the name. Fee is KES 150. Reservation lasts 30 days.
5. Complete the business name application. Fee is KES 1,050.
6. Provide your KRA PIN, ID, and physical address.
7. Wait 1 to 3 business days for approval.
8. Download your business registration certificate.

## Registering a limited company

For a limited company, the process is more involved:

1. Reserve the company name (same as above).
2. Prepare the memorandum and articles of association.
3. Provide details of directors and shareholders.
4. Pay the registration fee. It scales with share capital.
5. Wait for approval. Usually 3 to 7 business days.
6. Download the certificate of incorporation.

You will need a company secretary and a registered office address. Most startups hire a lawyer or accountant for this part.

## Getting your KRA PIN

Once registered, you need a KRA PIN for the business. This is separate from any personal PIN you already have.

Apply on iTax. You will need your business registration certificate, ID, and physical address. The PIN is usually issued within 48 hours.

## Registering for VAT

If your turnover will exceed KES 5 million in a 12-month period, register for VAT at the same time. There is no reason to delay this. Once you cross the threshold, registration is mandatory.

## Opening a bank account

Banks in Kenya require:

- Business registration certificate
- KRA PIN certificate
- ID of the owner or directors
- Physical address proof
- Business permit from the county

Counties issue single business permits annually. Every business operating in a Kenyan county needs one, even an online business, if it has any physical presence.

## What most people forget

Three things catch first-time business owners:

1. **County business permit.** You cannot legally trade without one. They renew annually.
2. **NHIF/SHIF and NSSF registration.** Required the moment you hire your first employee.
3. **VAT registration.** Voluntary below KES 5 million turnover, mandatory above it.

Registering is quick now. Staying compliant is where the real work begins.
