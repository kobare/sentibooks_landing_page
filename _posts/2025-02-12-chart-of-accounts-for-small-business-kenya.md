The chart of accounts is the backbone of your bookkeeping. Every transaction gets coded to one of these accounts, and every report is built from them. Get it wrong at the start and every month-end becomes a puzzle.

## The five account types

Every chart of accounts, in any country, follows the same five-category structure:

1. **Assets.** What the business owns. Cash, bank balances, M-Pesa float, receivables, inventory, equipment.
2. **Liabilities.** What the business owes. Supplier payables, VAT payable, PAYE payable, loans.
3. **Equity.** Owner's stake. Capital contributed, retained earnings.
4. **Income.** Money earned. Sales revenue, service income, other income.
5. **Expenses.** Money spent on running the business. Salaries, rent, utilities, cost of goods.

The relationship between them is fixed by double-entry accounting. Every transaction affects at least two accounts, and assets minus liabilities always equals equity.

## Kenyan-specific accounts you must have

A generic chart of accounts imported from a foreign template will not serve you. You need accounts for:

- **VAT Input** (asset or contra-liability). Tracks VAT you have paid on purchases.
- **VAT Output** (liability). Tracks VAT you have charged on sales.
- **PAYE Payable.** Withheld from employees, waiting to be remitted to KRA.
- **SHIF Payable.** Deducted from employees, remitted to SHA.
- **NSSF Payable.** Both employee and employer shares.
- **Housing Levy Payable.** Deducted and matched by employer.
- **M-Pesa Float.** A dedicated asset account for money held in M-Pesa.
- **Bank Accounts.** One per bank account, not a single combined account.

If your accounting software is Kenyan-built, these are all included out of the box. If not, you will need to add them manually.

## Numbering convention

Use a numbering scheme so accounts sort logically:

- 1000 to 1999: Assets
- 2000 to 2999: Liabilities
- 3000 to 3999: Equity
- 4000 to 4999: Income
- 5000 to 5999: Expenses

Then sub-number within each range. For example:

- 1010 Bank Account
- 1020 M-Pesa Float
- 1030 Petty Cash
- 1100 Accounts Receivable
- 1200 Inventory

This makes reports readable and searchable.

## Keep it small at first

New business owners often build 200-account charts because they want to be thorough. This slows bookkeeping down and hides patterns in the reports.

Start with 25 to 40 accounts. Add new ones only when you have a real need to separate one category from another.

For example, start with a single "Utilities" expense account. If your electricity bill grows large enough that you want to track it separately from water, split it then.
