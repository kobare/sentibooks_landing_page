Small businesses lose weeks of cash flow every year to invoices that get rejected by corporate accounts departments. Most rejections are for reasons that are entirely avoidable.

## What corporate clients require on every invoice

Kenyan corporations follow strict invoice standards, partly for VAT compliance and partly because their accounts payable team cannot process anything that does not fit their format.

At minimum, every invoice must include:

- **Your business name and full physical address**
- **Your KRA PIN**, displayed prominently
- **The client's business name and KRA PIN**
- **Invoice number** in a format the client has approved
- **Invoice date and due date**
- **Detailed line items** describing what was delivered
- **Net amount, VAT amount, and gross amount** clearly separated
- **Payment terms** (e.g. 30 days from invoice date)
- **Bank account or M-Pesa details** for payment
- **A purchase order number** if the client issued one

Missing any of these is the number one reason invoices sit unpaid for 60 days.

## Common rejection reasons

Based on feedback from accounts teams at Kenyan corporates, here are the top reasons invoices get returned:

1. **Missing LPO number.** If the client issued a Local Purchase Order, the invoice must reference it.
2. **Wrong client KRA PIN.** Copy it exactly from their documents, including the letter.
3. **VAT calculation errors.** A 50-shilling discrepancy is enough to trigger a return.
4. **No signature or stamp.** Many corporates still expect a wet signature.
5. **Wrong bank details.** A mismatch between invoice and vendor registration fails verification.
6. **No delivery note attached.** For goods, this is mandatory in most corporates.

## Get on the approved vendor list first

Most corporates will not pay an invoice from a supplier who is not on their approved vendor list. This list exists for two reasons: fraud prevention and tax compliance.

To get on it, provide:

- Certificate of business registration
- KRA PIN certificate
- Tax compliance certificate
- Bank account details on official letterhead
- Any relevant licences for your industry

The process takes 1 to 4 weeks. Start it before you deliver the work, not after.

## Payment terms

Standard Kenyan corporate terms are 30 to 45 days. Some large companies push for 60 or 90.

Two things to negotiate:

- **Shorten the terms.** 30 days instead of 60 is worth asking for, especially on your first contract.
- **Partial upfront.** For larger engagements, request 30 to 50 percent on signature. Most corporates will agree to this.

If you cannot shorten terms, plan your cash flow around them. A business that runs out of cash while waiting for payment is a business that closes.

## Follow up professionally

If an invoice is past due, follow up in writing. Keep it short and factual:

> Dear [Accounts Contact], just following up on invoice [number] dated [date] for KES [amount]. The payment was due on [date]. Could you confirm the expected payment date? Thanks.

Escalate after 14 days overdue. Most corporates respond to a polite email from the business owner more than to one from an assistant.
