Every Kenyan business that owns equipment, vehicles, or furniture needs a fixed asset register. Most do not have one. The result is tax deductions that go unclaimed and replacement planning that happens in panic.

## What counts as a fixed asset

A fixed asset is something the business owns, uses for more than one year, and does not sell to customers. For a typical Kenyan SME, this usually includes:

- Computers, printers, and office equipment
- Furniture and fittings
- Vehicles
- Machinery and tools
- Software licences (if perpetual, not subscription)
- Leasehold improvements (renovations to a rented space)

Consumables like stationery and cleaning supplies are expenses, not assets.

## Why you need a register

An asset register does five things:

1. **Tracks what you own.** Without this, assets get lost, stolen, or forgotten.
2. **Records purchase date and cost.** Needed for depreciation calculations.
3. **Calculates depreciation for tax.** KRA allows capital deductions at specific rates.
4. **Schedules maintenance.** Every asset needs periodic servicing.
5. **Plans replacement and budget.** You cannot save for what you do not know you need.

## Depreciation rates in Kenya

KRA allows capital deductions at the following rates under the Second Schedule of the Income Tax Act:

- **Computer hardware and software:** 33.3 percent per year (3-year life)
- **Heavy machinery and earth-moving equipment:** 25 percent per year
- **Motor vehicles, plant, and other machinery:** 25 percent per year
- **Furniture, fixtures, and office equipment:** 12.5 percent per year (8-year life)
- **Industrial buildings:** 10 percent per year (Industrial Building Deduction)
- **Hotels and commercial buildings:** 10 percent per year (on eligible expenditure)

Small businesses with turnover below KES 5 million per year can elect to claim a simplified investment deduction on some asset categories. Consult your accountant on whether this applies to you.

## What goes in the register

For each asset, record:

- Description
- Asset number (unique ID)
- Location (which office, branch, or department)
- Purchase date
- Supplier
- Purchase cost
- Depreciation rate
- Accumulated depreciation to date
- Net book value
- Maintenance schedule
- Disposal date (when relevant)

## The subtle rules

Three things catch small businesses:

1. **Depreciation starts when the asset is put into use**, not when it is purchased. An asset bought in December but only used from March cannot be depreciated for the December to March period.
2. **Disposal gains and losses** must be recorded. If you sell an asset for more than its net book value, the difference is taxable income.
3. **Asset revaluation** for accounting purposes is separate from tax depreciation. Do not mix the two.

## Physical verification

Once a year, walk through your premises and physically verify every asset is where it should be. Anything missing needs an investigation.

An asset register that is not verified annually drifts out of accuracy within a couple of years. And an inaccurate register is worse than none, because it gives false confidence.
