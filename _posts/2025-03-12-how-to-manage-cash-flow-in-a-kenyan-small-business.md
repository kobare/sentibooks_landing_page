Profitable businesses close every week in Kenya. Not because they are losing money on paper, but because they run out of cash. Cash flow and profit are two different things, and understanding the difference is what keeps a business alive.

## Why profit does not equal cash

You invoice a client KES 500,000 on 30-day terms. Your bookkeeping shows profit the day you invoice. But you do not have the cash.

In the meantime, you pay salaries, rent, and supplier bills. If the client pays in 60 days instead of 30, you need enough cash on hand to cover that extra month.

Three things create the gap between profit and cash:

1. **Receivables.** Money clients owe you but have not paid.
2. **Inventory.** Cash tied up in stock sitting on shelves.
3. **Prepayments.** Money paid out before you recognise the expense.

## The three levers

You manage cash flow with three levers:

**Speed up cash in.**

- Invoice immediately on delivery, not at month end
- Offer a 2 percent discount for payment within 7 days
- Request 30 to 50 percent upfront on larger jobs
- Send polite reminders on day 25, 35, and 45 for 30-day terms

**Slow down cash out.**

- Negotiate 45 to 60 day terms with your main suppliers
- Stagger payments on large bills instead of paying all at once
- Do not pay early unless there is a discount worth taking

**Reduce the gap.**

- Hold less inventory. Order smaller, more frequently.
- Use your personal network for short-term bridging rather than expensive bank overdrafts
- Keep a cash buffer of at least 4 weeks of operating expenses

## Forecast weekly, not monthly

Monthly forecasts hide the real cash position. The trouble happens in week 3, not at month end.

Build a simple weekly cash forecast:

| Week | Opening | Cash in | Cash out | Closing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | 300 | 150 | 180 | 270 |
| W2 | 270 | 100 | 200 | 170 |
| W3 | 170 | 400 | 150 | 420 |
| W4 | 420 | 50 | 300 | 170 |

If any week shows a negative closing balance, you need to plan for it now, not when it arrives.

## The M-Pesa factor

M-Pesa moves in and out of your business faster than bank transactions. This is good for liquidity but hard to track.

Two rules:

1. **Reconcile M-Pesa weekly**, not monthly. The float position changes daily.
2. **Keep the business M-Pesa separate from personal.** Mixing them destroys both your books and your cash visibility.

## Warning signs

Watch for these early warnings:

- You are paying suppliers late but customers are paying you on time
- You keep "borrowing" from next month's expected income
- Your bank balance feels tight even in a strong revenue month
- You are dipping into VAT or PAYE money to pay operating expenses

The last one is a red line. Do not spend money that is earmarked for KRA. It never ends well.
