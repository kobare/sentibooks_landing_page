Inventory is the biggest cash trap in retail. Too little and you lose sales. Too much and your money sits on a shelf, aging. The skill is finding the middle.

## The two enemies

**Stockouts** cost you sales today. A customer walks in for a specific item and walks out with nothing. In most cases, they do not come back.

**Dead stock** costs you cash tomorrow. Money spent on inventory that does not move is money you cannot spend on anything else. It also takes up shelf space that could hold faster-moving items.

Healthy retailers aim for stock turnover of 6 to 12 times a year, depending on the category.

## Track everything by SKU

A SKU (Stock Keeping Unit) is a unique code for every distinct item you sell. Not "shoes" but "black leather shoe, size 42". Not "phone charger" but "USB-C fast charger, white".

Without SKU-level tracking, you cannot tell which items sell and which sit. You only see the category total, which hides the truth.

## Set reorder points

For each SKU, set a reorder point: the stock level at which you place a new order.

The formula:

**Reorder point = (Average daily sales × Lead time in days) + Safety stock**

Example: You sell 5 units of an item per day. Your supplier takes 4 days to deliver. You want 3 days of safety stock.

Reorder point = (5 × 4) + (5 × 3) = 20 + 15 = 35 units.

When stock hits 35, place an order.

## ABC analysis

Not all SKUs deserve equal attention. Sort them by revenue contribution:

- **A items** (top 20 percent of SKUs, ~80 percent of revenue): count weekly, reorder frequently, never allow stockouts.
- **B items** (next 30 percent, ~15 percent of revenue): count monthly, standard reorder process.
- **C items** (bottom 50 percent, ~5 percent of revenue): count quarterly, consider discontinuing.

Most retailers over-manage C items and under-manage A items. Flip that.

## Handling dead stock

If an item has not sold in 90 days, act:

1. **Discount 20 to 30 percent.** Better to recover most of the cost than hold it forever.
2. **Bundle with a fast-moving item.** Attach it to something that sells.
3. **Move to a secondary channel.** Wholesale, online marketplaces, or a partner retailer.
4. **Write it off.** After 12 months, the tax deduction is worth more than the small chance of eventually selling it at full price.

Dead stock is not free because it "might sell one day". It costs you storage, shelf space, and opportunity.

## Physical counts

Digital records drift. Employees miscount. Items get damaged or stolen.

Do:
- **Weekly count of A items.** Fast, focused, catches problems early.
- **Monthly count of B items.**
- **Full stocktake quarterly** if you can afford the disruption, annually at minimum.

Investigate every variance above KES 1,000 the same week. Not later. Later never happens.
