A KRA audit is not a punishment. It is a review. But if your records are scattered across notebooks, WhatsApp screenshots, and half-finished spreadsheets, a routine review can turn into a nightmare.

## What triggers an audit

KRA does not audit every business. They focus on patterns that suggest under-declaration:

1. **Consistent nil VAT returns** while the business appears active (you have employees, you have a physical location, you advertise).
2. **Income tax declared is lower than what M-Pesa and bank data show.** KRA can and does cross-check these.
3. **Turnover near the VAT threshold but not registered.** If you are at KES 4.8 million and not VAT-registered, expect a letter.
4. **Large or unusual refund claims.** If you claim more input VAT than output VAT consistently, that is a pattern.
5. **Employee complaints.** A former employee reporting unpaid statutory deductions triggers a payroll audit.

Any one of these alone can be explained. Two or more together is a strong signal.

## The seven records you need

If an auditor walked in tomorrow, you should be able to produce these within 24 hours:

1. **Sales ledger.** Every invoice issued, with invoice numbers, dates, customer details, amounts, and VAT.
2. **Purchase ledger.** Every supplier invoice and receipt, with supplier PIN.
3. **VAT returns filed** for the last 3 to 5 years.
4. **Bank and M-Pesa statements** for the same period.
5. **Payroll records.** P10 returns, payslips, and statutory remittance confirmations.
6. **Fixed asset register.**
7. **Financial statements** for each year in scope.

If any of these take more than a day to assemble, you have work to do before the audit arrives, not during it.

## During the audit

Three rules:

**Do not volunteer information.** Answer the questions asked. Do not speculate, do not explain beyond the scope, do not offer access to anything not specifically requested.

**Do not argue.** If you disagree with the auditor's finding, say so politely and provide documentation. Arguments escalate.

**Do not delay responding.** Every request has a deadline. Meet it.

## Common audit outcomes

Most audits end in one of these:

- **No adjustment.** Records were clean. Case closed.
- **Small adjustment.** Some input VAT disallowed, or some expenses rejected. Pay the difference plus penalties.
- **Assessment for under-declared income.** Larger issue. Usually involves negotiation with KRA's assessment team.
- **Criminal referral.** Rare, and usually reserved for cases involving forged documents or deliberate evasion.

Most audits fall in the first two categories. Being ready keeps you there.

## Staying ready

The best audit preparation is not something you do when the letter arrives. It is what you do every month.

- Reconcile bank and M-Pesa to your books monthly
- File VAT, PAYE, and annual returns on time, every time
- Keep invoices and receipts for 5 years minimum, 7 years preferred
- Store everything digitally so you can find any document in seconds

If you do those four things, an audit becomes a formality.
