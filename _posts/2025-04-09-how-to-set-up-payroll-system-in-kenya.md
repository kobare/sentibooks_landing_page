Setting up payroll for the first time feels overwhelming. There are statutory registrations, deduction calculations, and monthly filing deadlines. Get the structure right once and it becomes routine.

## Before you run your first payroll

Five things must be in place:

1. **Registered as an employer with KRA.** This is separate from your business PIN. It gives you an Employer PIN for PAYE filing.
2. **Registered with NSSF** as an employer. You will need a registration certificate and your employees' NSSF numbers.
3. **Registered with SHA** (Social Health Authority) for SHIF.
4. **Registered with the Housing Levy** via KRA (usually rolled into your existing KRA employer account).
5. **Written employment contracts** for every employee, specifying salary, terms, and statutory deductions.

Registration takes 1 to 2 weeks if you have all documents ready. Do it before you hire, not after.

## Employee data to collect

For each employee, gather:

- Full name and ID number
- KRA PIN
- NSSF number
- SHIF number
- Bank account or M-Pesa number for salary payment
- Employment start date
- Gross salary and any allowances
- Emergency contact

Store this securely. It is personal data under the Data Protection Act.

## Structure the pay

A typical Kenyan payslip has these components:

**Earnings:**
- Basic salary
- House allowance
- Transport allowance
- Other allowances (airtime, medical, etc.)

**Statutory deductions:**
- PAYE (progressive, based on taxable income)
- SHIF (2.75 percent of gross)
- NSSF (6 percent employee plus 6 percent employer, two tiers)
- Housing Levy (1.5 percent employee plus 1.5 percent employer)

**Other deductions:**
- Salary advances
- Staff loans
- SACCO contributions
- Union dues (if applicable)

**Net pay** is what the employee receives.

## Taxable vs non-taxable allowances

Most allowances are taxable. A few are not, within limits:

- **Non-taxable:** Certain reimbursements (mileage at KRA-approved rates, per diem below thresholds, medical insurance premiums up to KES 15,000 per month).
- **Taxable:** Most cash allowances.

Get this wrong and you under-withhold, which creates a liability you have to fix later.

## The monthly payroll cycle

Day 1 to 3 of the month:
- Collect timesheets for the previous month
- Confirm any leave taken, advances requested, or new hires

Day 4 to 6:
- Run the payroll calculation
- Review every payslip before sending

Day 7 to 8:
- Send payslips to employees
- Process salary payments via bank or M-Pesa

Day 9:
- Remit PAYE, SHIF, and Housing Levy to KRA
- Remit NSSF to NSSF
- File the P10 return

Day 10 onwards:
- Handle any payroll queries
- Prepare for next month

## Common mistakes

1. **Paying salaries before calculating deductions.** You end up with a deficit to cover.
2. **Missing the 9th deadline.** Penalties apply. Set recurring reminders.
3. **Forgetting the employer's NSSF and Housing Levy contributions.** These come out of the business, not the employee's salary.
4. **Not issuing payslips.** It is a legal requirement.
