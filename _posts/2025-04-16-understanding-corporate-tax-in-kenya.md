If your business is registered as a limited company in Kenya, corporate tax is one of the largest single line items on your P&L. Understanding how it works is worth real money.

## The rate

Resident companies pay **30 percent** of taxable profit. Non-resident companies with a permanent establishment in Kenya pay **37.5 percent**.

Small companies with turnover below KES 5 million a year and no employees may qualify for a lower rate if they fall under the Turnover Tax regime. This is optional and only applies to a subset of businesses.

## Taxable profit, not accounting profit

Corporate tax is calculated on taxable profit, which is different from accounting profit. Two adjustments do most of the work:

**Add back non-deductible expenses.** Examples:
- Fines and penalties
- Personal expenses run through the business
- Some entertainment costs
- Capital expenditure (this becomes a capital deduction instead)

**Deduct capital allowances.** KRA gives tax deductions on qualifying capital expenditure:
- Computer hardware and software: 33.3 percent per year
- Vehicles and equipment: 25 percent per year
- Furniture and fixtures: 12.5 percent per year
- Industrial buildings: 10 percent per year

These tax deductions are separate from accounting depreciation.

## What you can deduct

Most genuine business expenses are deductible if they are wholly and exclusively incurred for the business:

- Salaries and wages (including statutory contributions)
- Rent, utilities, and insurance
- Cost of goods sold
- Professional fees
- Marketing and advertising
- Interest on business loans
- Bank charges
- Repairs and maintenance

You need receipts or invoices for every one of them.

## What you cannot deduct

- Personal expenses of the owner or directors
- Fines, penalties, and taxes on income
- Donations to non-approved bodies
- Capital expenditure in the year of purchase (this goes through capital allowances instead)
- Most entertainment expenses

The line between business and personal expenses is where most small business audits go wrong. If a purchase benefits you personally and the business, do not put it in the books.

## Filing deadlines

For companies with a December year-end:

- **Provisional tax:** Two payments, one by the 20th day of the 4th month and one by the 20th day of the 10th month of the accounting period.
- **Final return:** Within 6 months of the financial year end (30 June for a December year-end).

Late filing triggers a penalty of KES 20,000 or 5 percent of the tax due, whichever is higher, plus interest.

## Provisional vs final

Provisional tax is a best-estimate prepayment based on your expected profit for the year. The final return reconciles actual profit against provisional payments. If you overpaid, you get credit. If you underpaid, you settle.

The rule of thumb: provisional payments must be at least 110 percent of the prior year's tax, or the actual tax due, whichever is lower. Pay attention to this or you will get an assessment.

## Where businesses get caught

Three patterns:

1. **Expenses without documentation.** A KES 50,000 deduction without a proper receipt is a KES 50,000 deduction that will be disallowed.
2. **Mixing personal and business.** Payments for personal items through the business account. Even if you "intended" to reimburse, KRA does not see intent, they see transactions.
3. **Underpaying provisional tax.** The reconciliation at year end creates a large balance due plus penalties.
