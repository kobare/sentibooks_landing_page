Every shilling you spend on the business should be recorded, categorised, and verifiable. Most businesses lose track of 3 to 8 percent of expenses, which either becomes untracked profit or a disallowed tax deduction.

## The three-part system

Effective expense tracking has three parts:

1. **Capture.** Every expense gets recorded the moment it happens.
2. **Categorise.** Each expense is coded to the correct account.
3. **Review.** Every month, you look at the numbers and ask what they mean.

The first part is where most businesses fail.

## Capture in real time

If you wait until end of month to record expenses from a stack of receipts, you will lose some. Guaranteed.

Capture options:

- **Take a photo of every receipt the moment you get it.** Store in a dedicated album or folder.
- **Log the expense in your accounting app in real time.** Most modern apps have a mobile version and receipt scan feature.
- **Use a dedicated expense card.** Every purchase lands on one statement that flows into your books monthly.

Whichever you choose, the rule is the same: **capture at the point of spend**, not later.

## Categorise consistently

Every expense goes into one of your chart-of-accounts categories. The categories should be:

- **Broad enough** that you do not create new ones constantly
- **Specific enough** that the reports tell you something

Typical categories for a Kenyan SME:

- Rent
- Utilities (electricity, water, internet)
- Salaries and wages
- Statutory contributions (NSSF, SHIF, Housing Levy employer share)
- Office supplies
- Travel and transport
- Marketing and advertising
- Professional fees
- Bank charges and M-Pesa fees
- Repairs and maintenance
- Insurance

When in doubt, put it in the closest category. You can re-categorise later if needed.

## Separate business and personal

Every shilling spent from the business account on personal items must be recorded as a **director's draw** or **owner's draw**, not as a business expense.

This is the single biggest source of tax trouble for small Kenyan businesses. KRA does not care that you "intended" to reimburse the business. If the transaction shows a personal purchase through the business account without documentation, it gets disallowed.

Two ways to keep them clean:

1. **Use a dedicated business account and card.** Never spend business money on personal items.
2. **If you must, log it as a draw immediately.** Do not hide it as "miscellaneous expenses".

## Review monthly

At the end of each month, look at:

- Total expenses by category
- Month-over-month change by category
- Any category that jumped by more than 20 percent

Ask three questions about anything unusual:

1. Was this expense necessary?
2. Can it be reduced next month?
3. Is it being categorised correctly?

Fifteen minutes of review every month prevents 90 percent of the surprises that show up at year end.

## Digital vs paper

Paper receipts degrade. If KRA audits you in three years, the receipts from today will have faded. Take photos of every receipt.

For digital expense tracking, the workflow is:

1. Expense happens
2. Receipt photographed or scanned
3. Expense logged in the accounting system with the image attached
4. Original receipt filed (if paper) or discarded (if digital)
