Most business owners look at their bank balance and call it "knowing the numbers". That is like a doctor looking at your heart rate and calling it a diagnosis.

There are five financial reports that, read together, tell you the truth about your business.

## 1. Profit and Loss (Income Statement)

**What it shows:** Revenue, costs, and profit over a period.

**What to look for:**
- Gross margin percentage. Is it holding steady or drifting?
- Expense growth vs revenue growth. If expenses grow faster than revenue, you have a structural problem.
- Month-over-month trend. One bad month is noise. Three bad months is a trend.

**Read it:** Monthly, without exception.

## 2. Balance Sheet

**What it shows:** What the business owns (assets), owes (liabilities), and its net worth (equity) at a point in time.

**What to look for:**
- Cash position. Is it trending up or down?
- Receivables. How much are customers sitting on?
- Payables. How much do you owe suppliers?
- Debt-to-equity ratio. Are you over-leveraged?

**Read it:** Monthly or at least quarterly.

## 3. Cash Flow Statement

**What it shows:** How cash moved in and out of the business across operations, investing, and financing.

**What to look for:**
- Operating cash flow. Is it positive? If not, the business is burning cash to operate.
- Working capital changes. Is cash tied up in inventory or receivables?
- Financing activities. Are you borrowing to fund operating losses? That is a warning sign.

**Read it:** Monthly. This is the report that tells you whether the business survives.

## 4. Aged Receivables

**What it shows:** How much customers owe you, grouped by how long they have owed it (0-30 days, 31-60, 61-90, 90+).

**What to look for:**
- Anything in the 90+ column. That is money you may not collect.
- Concentration. If one customer is 40 percent of your receivables, you have a risk.

**Read it:** Weekly.

## 5. Aged Payables

**What it shows:** How much you owe suppliers, grouped by age.

**What to look for:**
- Anything overdue. Late payment damages supplier relationships.
- Cash you have on hand vs upcoming payables. Can you cover next week's payments?

**Read it:** Weekly.

## The five-minute weekly check

Every Monday morning, look at three things:

1. Bank and M-Pesa balance
2. Aged receivables
3. Aged payables

If any of the three feels off, dig into the underlying reports before the week gets away from you.

## The monthly deep dive

Once a month, review:

- P&L vs budget (or vs last month)
- Balance sheet movement
- Cash flow statement
- Top 5 customers by revenue and by outstanding balance
- Top 5 expense categories

Fifteen minutes if you have the data organized. Two hours if you do not.

## Why this matters

Business owners who read these five reports regularly spot problems while they are small. Problems spotted early are fixed with a decision. Problems spotted late are fixed with money.
