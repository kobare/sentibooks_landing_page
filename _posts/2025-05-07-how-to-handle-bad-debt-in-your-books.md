Every business eventually has a customer who will not pay. The invoice sits in your receivables, aging, until you accept that the money is not coming.

How you handle it in your books matters both for accuracy and for tax.

## The three stages

Bad debt moves through three stages:

**Stage 1: Overdue.** The invoice is past its due date. Most businesses at this stage just send reminders.

**Stage 2: Doubtful.** You suspect it will not be collected, but you are not ready to write it off. You may make a provision for doubtful debt on your books.

**Stage 3: Bad.** You have exhausted reasonable collection efforts. The debt is written off.

## Before you write off

Do not write off a debt until you have:

1. Sent at least three written reminders over 60 days
2. Called and spoken to someone at the company
3. Sent a formal demand letter, ideally on letterhead
4. Considered small claims court if the amount justifies it (KES 20,000 to 1 million qualifies)
5. Confirmed the company has not gone into administration or liquidation (which would make recovery unlikely)

Keep records of every attempt. You will need them for tax purposes.

## How to write it off

The journal entry is:

- **Debit:** Bad Debt Expense (an expense account)
- **Credit:** Accounts Receivable (the specific customer)

This removes the receivable from your books and recognises the loss.

If you had previously made a provision for doubtful debt, you reverse the provision instead.

## Tax treatment

**VAT:** When you originally issued the invoice, you charged VAT. You remitted that VAT to KRA. If the customer does not pay, you can claim a **bad debt VAT adjustment** to recover the VAT you remitted, provided:

- The debt has been outstanding for over 12 months
- You have written off the debt in your books
- You have documentation showing you attempted collection

The claim is filed on your VAT3 return as a deduction from output VAT.

**Income tax:** Bad debts you write off are deductible against taxable income, provided:

- The debt was previously included in income
- It is genuinely irrecoverable
- You have documentation

Loans to related parties and debts that were never income in the first place are not deductible.

## What not to do

Three things to avoid:

1. **Do not write off a debt while still pursuing it.** Pick one. If you are still chasing, keep it on the books.
2. **Do not write off a debt and then collect it without recording the collection.** The collection becomes income in the year received.
3. **Do not "write off" personal debts through the business.** KRA will disallow and may assess penalties.

## Preventing bad debt

The best bad debt strategy is preventing it:

- For new customers, request payment upfront or on delivery
- For ongoing credit customers, set credit limits and monitor them
- Run a simple check: ask for two trade references on any credit customer
- Invoice immediately. Delay increases risk.
- Follow up on day 1 past due, not day 30.
