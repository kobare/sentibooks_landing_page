Small daily expenses add up. Airtime, fuel, cleaning supplies, courier fees. If they come out of the owner's pocket and get reimbursed from memory, they disappear from your books and from your tax deductions.

Petty cash solves this. Set it up properly and it takes 10 minutes a week.

## What petty cash is for

Petty cash is for small, immediate expenses that cannot wait for a bank transfer or supplier invoice. Typically anything under KES 5,000.

It is not for:
- Salary advances (those go through payroll)
- Personal spending
- Large purchases (those go through proper procurement)
- Anything you can pay by M-Pesa or bank

## The imprest system

The simplest effective petty cash system is called **imprest**. Here is how it works:

1. Set a fixed float. Say KES 20,000.
2. Custodian (the person managing the cash) keeps this in a lockable box.
3. When cash is spent, custodian records the expense and attaches the receipt.
4. When the float drops to half, the custodian requests replenishment.
5. Finance reimburses the exact amount spent, restoring the float to KES 20,000.

The float always returns to the original amount. You can audit it any time by counting the cash plus receipts.

## The petty cash voucher

Every single payment from petty cash needs a voucher. No exceptions.

A petty cash voucher records:

- Date
- Voucher number (sequential)
- Amount
- What it was for
- Who received the money
- Who approved it
- Signature of recipient

Attach the receipt to the back of the voucher. No receipt, no payment. If a vendor cannot issue a receipt (a matatu ride, for example), the recipient writes a simple note explaining the expense.

## The petty cash book

Every entry in the petty cash book mirrors a voucher. It shows:

| Voucher # | Date | Description | Paid | Balance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 001 | 01/05 | Airtime for office | 500 | 19,500 |
| 002 | 02/05 | Fuel for delivery van | 3,000 | 16,500 |
| 003 | 03/05 | Courier to Mombasa | 800 | 15,700 |

At the end of each week, the balance in the book should match the cash in the box.

## Monthly reconciliation

At month end:

1. Count the cash in the box
2. Add up the total of vouchers
3. Cash + vouchers should equal the original float
4. Record all vouchers in your accounting software
5. Replenish to restore the float

If the numbers do not balance, investigate before replenishing. Discrepancies get worse if you ignore them.

## Who manages petty cash

The custodian should not be the same person who approves expenses or reconciles the account. Separation of duties matters, even in a small business.

In a 3-person business, the person who runs petty cash is different from the person who signs off on the monthly reconciliation. Even if the owner approves everything, someone else should hold the box.

## KRA implications

Every petty cash expense is a business expense and should be recorded. For VAT-registered businesses, small cash expenses usually do not have recoverable VAT, but the base expense is still deductible against income tax.

Without receipts, none of this is provable. Receipts or vouchers, every time.
