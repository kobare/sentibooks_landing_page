Almost every Kenyan business needs at least one bank account. But banks differ significantly in fees, minimum balances, and the services they bundle in. Choosing the wrong one costs you money every month.

## What to compare

Six factors:

1. **Monthly account maintenance fee.** Ranges from KES 200 to KES 2,000 per month.
2. **Minimum balance.** Some accounts require KES 5,000, others KES 50,000, some zero.
3. **Transaction fees.** Per deposit, per withdrawal, per transfer. These add up faster than the monthly fee.
4. **M-Pesa integration.** Whether you can link a business M-Pesa till directly to the account.
5. **Online and mobile banking.** Quality of the app, ease of bulk payments, real-time balances.
6. **Access to credit.** Whether the bank offers business loans or overdrafts, and at what rates.

## The hidden fees

The monthly maintenance fee is not the whole story. Watch for:

- **Cash deposit fees.** Often 0.5 to 1 percent of the amount deposited.
- **Withdrawal fees.** Higher for over-the-counter than ATM.
- **Transfer fees.** Especially for inter-bank transfers.
- **Banker's cheque fees.** KES 500 to KES 1,500 each.
- **Statement fees.** Some banks charge for physical statements.
- **Account reactivation fees.** If your balance drops below minimum for a while.
- **Loan processing fees.** Usually 1 to 3 percent of the loan amount.

Ask for a full fee schedule before opening. If the bank will not provide one, that is your answer.

## M-Pesa is a bank account

For most Kenyan SMEs, M-Pesa is the primary day-to-day account. You should treat it as such.

Open a dedicated business M-Pesa line (not your personal line). Use the till number for customer payments and the paybill for supplier payments. The bank account handles larger transactions, payroll, and tax payments.

Two things to keep in mind:

1. **M-Pesa and bank accounts should not be mixed in your books.** Use separate accounts in your accounting software, then reconcile both.
2. **Reconcile M-Pesa weekly, bank monthly.** M-Pesa moves faster, so problems surface sooner.

## What to ask before opening

Five questions to ask every bank:

1. What is the total annual cost including all transaction fees, based on my typical monthly volume?
2. What is the process and timeline for opening the account? (Some take 1 day, some take 3 weeks.)
3. Is there a relationship manager assigned to my account?
4. What happens if my balance drops below the minimum? Fees? Suspension?
5. Can I set up automatic payroll payments, VAT payments, and standing orders?

The bank that answers these clearly and quickly is the one worth considering.

## Documents you will need

For a business account, banks require:

- Business registration certificate
- KRA PIN certificate
- ID of the account signatories
- Board resolution (for limited companies)
- Physical address proof (utility bill or lease)
- Business permit

For a sole proprietorship, the requirements are lighter but similar.

## Practical advice

Open accounts with two banks, not one. This gives you:

- Backup if one bank has an outage
- Leverage when negotiating fees
- Easier access to credit

Keep the primary account at the bank with the best app and the lowest overall fees for your transaction pattern. Keep the secondary account with lower minimum balance for backup.
