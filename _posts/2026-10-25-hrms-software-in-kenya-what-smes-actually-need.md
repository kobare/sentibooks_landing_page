Search for HRMS software in Kenya and you will find twenty vendors claiming to do everything. Recruitment. Performance reviews. Training management. Org charts. Employee engagement surveys. Succession planning.

Most Kenyan SMEs will use none of that. They will use payroll, leave, timesheets, and the ability to keep employee records in one place. That is it.

This guide is about the gap between what a full HRMS includes and what a growing Kenyan business actually needs.

## What a full HRMS actually is

An HRMS (Human Resource Management System) is designed to handle the entire employee lifecycle in one platform. In its full form it typically includes:

- **Recruitment.** Job postings, applicant tracking, interview scheduling, offer letters.
- **Onboarding.** New hire checklists, document collection, contract signing.
- **Core HR.** Employee records, organisational charts, reporting lines, document storage.
- **Time and attendance.** Clock-in systems, timesheets, shift scheduling.
- **Leave management.** Accrual tracking, approval workflows, balance reporting.
- **Payroll.** Salary processing, statutory deductions, payslip generation.
- **Performance management.** Goal setting, reviews, feedback, appraisals.
- **Training and development.** Course tracking, certifications, learning paths.
- **Compensation and benefits.** Salary benchmarking, bonus structures, benefits administration.
- **Employee engagement.** Surveys, feedback, recognition programmes.
- **Compliance.** Employment law compliance, statutory reporting, audit trails.
- **Reporting and analytics.** Headcount, turnover, cost analysis, workforce planning.

That is the full picture. It is what a 200-person company needs. It is what an HR department uses every day. It is also, for most Kenyan SMEs, overkill.

## What Kenyan SMEs actually use

Of the twelve modules above, most businesses with under 50 employees use five: core HR (employee records), time and attendance, leave, payroll, and compliance. Everything else sits unused, paid for and forgotten.

That is not a criticism of the businesses. It is a structural reality. The average Kenyan SME with 10 to 30 employees has one person doing HR, and that person is usually also doing payroll, admin, and often accounts. They do not have time to run performance reviews or training programmes. They need the parts of HR that stop the business from being fined, sued, or paying the wrong person the wrong amount.

The five modules that matter for a Kenyan SME:

**Employee records.** Contact details, ID number, KRA PIN, NSSF number, SHIF number, bank account, employment contract, start date. All in one place, searchable, secure.

**Timesheets.** Who worked what hours. Essential for casual staff and contract workers. Absence of timesheet data is the most common source of payroll disputes.

**Leave management.** Accrual tracking, approval workflow, balance reporting. Every employer in Kenya has a legal obligation to track annual leave, and manual tracking breaks the moment you have more than five employees.

**Payroll.** Salary processing, PAYE, SHIF, NSSF, and Housing Levy calculations, payslip generation, P10 filing.

**Statutory compliance.** Employment Act compliance, NITA registration, the annual NSSF and SHIF schedules, the records that KRA and the labour office expect to see if they ever come calling.

Everything else — recruitment, performance reviews, training, engagement surveys — is optional at this size and usually does more harm than good when implemented badly.

## Why buying a full HRMS usually fails for an SME

Three reasons.

**The unused modules create a false sense of coverage.** The business thinks it has a full HR function because the software does. Then nobody uses the performance review module, nobody runs the training programme, and the business has the same HR capability it started with, just at a higher monthly cost.

**Implementation time swamps the benefit.** A full HRMS takes weeks to configure and months to fully adopt. For a business with 15 employees, that is a staggering investment of time relative to what gets used.

**The features that matter end up buried.** When a platform tries to do everything, the payroll module is often less good than a dedicated payroll tool, the leave module is less good than a dedicated leave tool, and so on. The business ends up with mediocre versions of everything instead of one excellent tool for what it actually uses.

## What to actually look for

For a Kenyan SME, the evaluation criteria are not "does it have module X." They are:

**Does it handle Kenyan statutory deductions accurately?** PAYE bands, personal relief, SHIF at 2.75 percent, NSSF tiers, and Housing Levy at 1.5 percent employee plus 1.5 percent employer. If it does not, it will be wrong every month.

**Does it handle casual staff?** A large share of Kenyan SMEs employ casuals whose days change weekly. If the system assumes all staff are salaried, it will not work.

**Does it handle leave accrual per Kenyan employment law?** 21 working days of annual leave, maternity at three months, paternity at two weeks. The calculations should be built in, not configured by you.

**Does it handle employee records properly?** Not just contact details, but the statutory identifiers. KRA PIN, NSSF number, SHIF number, NITA registration.

**Does it produce the P10 and the statutory schedules?** These are the documents KRA and NSSF want monthly. If the system produces them, payroll takes minutes. If not, it takes a day.

**Is it priced for an SME?** A full HRMS priced at KES 50,000 a month for a 15-employee business is not the right product, no matter how good it is. The cost needs to match the value.
